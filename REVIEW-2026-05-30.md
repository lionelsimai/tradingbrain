## TL;DR — the three things that matter most

1. **The "live scorecard" is 99.9% replay, not live, and it's labelled "LIVE
   EDGE CONFIRMED."** Of 2,727 ledger rows, **3** are `source='live'`; 2,724 are
   `replay` (a re-run of the detector over recent history). The verdict string
   said *"LIVE EDGE CONFIRMED (+0.275R over 2,715 trades)."* That's a backtest,
   not a forward test. ✅ FIXED (honest relabeling).

2. **The learning loop compares two different trade simulators and calls the
   gap "edge decay."** `calibration.json` (from `stress_test.py`) uses a
   two-target scale-out exit. `live-scorecard.json` (from `signal_tracker.py`)
   uses the detector's single-target exit-all-at-T1. `reconcile.py` subtracts
   one from the other and auto-demotes setups to *Broken*. **Proven empirically:
   the same VCP signals over the same window score −0.018R (single-target) vs
   +0.372R (scale-out) — a +0.39R swing from the exit rule alone.** That is the
   entire magnitude of the "decay" that demoted VCP. 🔧 SHIPPED a single shared
   simulator (`backtest/trade_sim.py`) to make all engines measure the same
   thing; 📋 wire it in + retrain.

3. **No position-size cap.** The live engine sized a single NVDA position to
   **36.2% of equity** while the doctrine mandates ≤20%/position and ≤6%
   portfolio heat. Tight stops blow past the cap silently. ✅ FIXED.

## P0 — undermines a claim or risks capital

### P0.1 — "Live" scorecard is replay  ✅ FIXED
`loops/signal_tracker.py::backfill()` replays `detect_setup` point-in-time over
the last ~9 months and resolves each — useful, but it is an out-of-sample
*backtest*, not live fills. Yet `scorecard()` aggregated all sources together
and `_verdict()` printed *"LIVE EDGE CONFIRMED."* `PACKAGE_README.md` then
states *"the live scorecard is the source of truth, not the backtest… the
difference between a backtest and a learning system."* With 3 live trades, that
claim is not yet earned.

- **Evidence:** `export-state/signal_ledger.csv` → `source` counts:
  `{'live': 3, 'replay': 2724}`.
- **Fix applied:** `scorecard()` now splits `live` vs `replay`, reports
  `n_live`/`n_replay`, only says "LIVE" when ≥20 true-live trades exist, and
  otherwise emits *"REPLAY-BASED, NOT LIVE … accumulate live fills before
  trusting."* A `methodology_caveat` is written into the JSON.
- **Also update** `PACKAGE_README.md`'s "live scorecard is the source of truth"
  paragraph to say *replay-based recent-regime read* until live fills accrue.

### P0.2 — Apples-to-oranges drift detection  🔧 SHIPPED MODULE
There are **four** trade-plan/exit definitions for the same named setups:

| Engine | Plan / exit | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| `scripts/analyze.py` | structural stop, T1=swing-high, T2=measured move, 50% scale | live grades |
| `backtest/stress_test.py` | structural 2-target scale-out | `calibration.json` |
| `backtest/research_engine.py` | `min(swing_low−0.25ATR, entry−0.8ATR)`, 2-target scale | `research-report.json` |
| `loops/signal_tracker.py` | **detector's own** stop/target, **single** exit-all-at-T1 | `live-scorecard.json` |

So a single strategy name has three different expectancies on the same data:
**PULLBACK = +0.206R** (research_engine) vs **+0.669R** (calibration) vs
**+0.516R** (replay), and three different sample sizes (3272 / 541 / 57). They
were never comparable. `reconcile.py` computes `drift = live − backtest` across
this mismatch and demotes on it.

- **Empirical proof** (`VCP`, same signals, same window): single-target exit
  −0.018R / 35.8% win; scale-out exit +0.372R / 55.5% win → **+0.39R purely
  from the exit rule.** The live-scorecard's VCP = −0.427R that triggered the
  *Broken* demotion is dominated by this artifact, not regime decay.
- **Shipped:** `backtest/trade_sim.py` — one `build_plan` + one `simulate`
  (with `scale_out` flag and a shared `costs_to_R`). 
- **To do (📋):** import it in `stress_test.py`, `research_engine.py`, and
  `signal_tracker.py` (resolve + backfill), pick ONE canonical exit (the
  doctrine documents scale-out), re-run `reflection_weekly.py`. Then drift is
  signal, not noise. *Use* the single-target run only as an explicit
  sensitivity, clearly labelled.

### P0.3 — No position-size / heat cap  ✅ FIXED
`scripts/analyze.py` sized purely off 1R risk, so a tight stop → oversized
notional (NVDA: 36.2% of equity). DOCTRINE Part VI: ≤20%/position, heat ≤6%.

- **Fix applied:** added `max_position_pct` (default 20, in `config/session.yaml`).
  The sizer now caps notional, reports `size_capped` + `effective_risk_pct`, and
  prints e.g. *"⚠ capped at 20% max position — effective risk 0.55% (<1.0%)."*
  Verified: NVDA now 20.0% / 0.55% effective; AMD unaffected at 7.2% / 0.97%.
- **Still 📋:** portfolio-heat enforcement is per-trade only; nothing sums open
  risk across positions vs the 6% cap or the 6-position limit. Add a portfolio
  layer (read `config/holdings.yaml`) that rejects/raises a new entry when
  cumulative heat would breach 6% or count > `max_concurrent_positions`.

### P0.4 — Calibration & replay are gross of costs  📋 RECOMMENDED
`research_engine.py` applies a cost model (`costs_R`); `stress_test.py` and
`signal_tracker.py` apply **none**. So `calibration.json` expectancies (which
gate live trades) are pre-cost, while the doctrine demands net-of-cost edges.
At ~0.03R/round-trip and thin edges (TREND_LEADER +0.149R), costs are material.
- **Fix:** route all three through `trade_sim.costs_to_R(...)` from
  `session.yaml`. The shipped module already exposes it.

## P1 — correctness & statistical consistency

### P1.1 — Overlapping samples inflate significance
`stress_test.py` scans every ticker `range(250, len-1, 3)` with **no cooldown**;
TREND_LEADER fires on essentially every uptrending name every 3 bars →
**n=18,750** highly autocorrelated, overlapping trades. `research_engine.py` at
least does `i += 5` after a fill. Large n here is not independent evidence;
bootstrap CIs and the `min_trades_for_significance=100` gate are over-optimistic.
- **Fix:** add a per-ticker cooldown in `stress_test` (mirror research_engine),
  and/or block-bootstrap by ticker. Report effective sample size, not raw n.

### P1.2 — `by_grade` is meaningless
`backfill()` hardcodes `grade="B"` for every replay signal, so
`live-scorecard.json::by_grade` only ever has a `B` bucket (n=2715). The grade
A/B/C distribution — which is what `analyze.py` actually decides on — is never
forward-tested. Either run replay through the real grader or drop `by_grade`.

### P1.3 — R/R is gameable by pushing targets out
Grade A/B needs `blended_rr ≥ 3`, trivially met because T2 = measured-move /
52w-extension sits far away (NVDA blended R/R = **5.11**). Distance ≠
probability-of-touch. A setup gets an A for a target it rarely reaches.
- **Fix:** grade on **expectancy** = `Σ p(hit)·R`, using the calibrated win
  rate / MFE distribution, not raw R/R. You already store `mfe_R`/`mae_R` in the
  ledger — use them to estimate P(touch T1/T2).

### P1.4 — Two regime classifiers can disagree silently
`stress_test.regime_at` (MA200 + 20d vol) and `research_engine.regime_at`
(MA200 + dd60 + ret60 + atrp) and `analyze._structural_regime` are three
different definitions. Per-regime tables across reports aren't comparable. Pick
one regime function (put it in a shared module) and import everywhere.

### P1.5 — `is_enabled` defaults True for unknown setups
`calibration.is_enabled` returns True when a setup has no calibration row
("graceful before first stress test"). Combined with `size_cap`'s
`Unknown→1.0`, a brand-new, never-validated setup trades at full size. Safer
default: unknown → disabled (or half), opt-in once validated.

### P1.6 — Doc/data drift: crypto
`PACKAGE_README.md` says "US equities only (crypto removed)" and
`prices.duckdb` has **0** `-USD` tickers — but `AGENTS.md` still claims "11
crypto", and `research_engine.py` / `stress_test.py` still branch on
`crypto_majors`/`-USD`, and `desk_signals.py` still polled `BTC-USD/ETH-USD/
SOL-USD` (plus non-existent `NBIS`/`CRWV`). ✅ FIXED desk pool (now filtered to
DB tickers). 📋 Prune crypto branches + reconcile `AGENTS.md`.

## P2 — performance & polish

- **P2.1 — `stress_test.py` is O(n²) and slow** (did not finish 78 tickers in
  300s). It recomputes rolling indicators on the full slice every 3 bars and
  reopens a DuckDB connection per ticker. Precompute indicators once per ticker
  (vectorized), reuse one connection, and iterate index positions. Same for
  `research_engine.load_prices` (connection per ticker × per strategy).
- **P2.2 — `datetime.utcnow()` deprecation** in `research_engine.py`. ✅ FIXED
  (→ `datetime.now(timezone.utc)`).
- **P2.3 — Hardcoded `ROOT = /home/workspace/TradingBrain`** in ~every file.
  Fine in production, breaks the "portable bundle" promise if unzipped
  elsewhere. Read from an env var (`TRADINGBRAIN_ROOT`) with that default.
- **P2.4 — `desk_signals.py` shells out** to `python -m scripts.analyze` once
  per ticker (28 subprocess launches). Import and call `analyze()` directly.
- **P2.5 — Live-vs-replay management mismatch.** `analyze.py` says "Move stop to
  breakeven after T1, trail under MA20"; the sims approximate trailing with a
  20-day timeout-to-close. Fine, but document that realized backtest R does not
  model the MA20 trail, so live and backtest exits differ on winners too.
